\poemtitle{Vertue}
\poemblock{Sweet spring, full of sweet dayes and roses,}
\begin{poem}[Sweet day, so cool, so calm, so bright,]
Sweet day, so cool, so calm, so bright,\\
The bridall of the earth and skie:\\
The dew shall weep thy fall to night;\\
\vin For thou must die.\\

Sweet rose, whose hue angrie and brave\\
Bids the rash gazer wipe his eye:\\
Thy root is ever in its grave,\\
\vin And thou must die.\\

Sweet spring, full of sweet dayes and roses,\\
A box where sweets compacted lie;\\
My musick shows ye have your closes,\\
\vin And all must die.\\

Onely a sweet and vertuous soul,\\
Like season'd timber, never gives;\\
But though the whole world turn to coal,\\
\vin Then chiefly lives.\\
\end{poem}

\poemtitle{The Pulley}
\poemblock{Then beautie flow'd, then wisdome, honour, pleasure:}
\begin{poem}[When God at first made man,]
When God at first made man,\\
Having a glasse of blessings standing by;\\
Let us (said he) poure on him all we can:\\
Let the worlds riches, which dispersed lie,\\
\vin Contract into a span.\\

So strength first made a way;\\
Then beautie flow'd, then wisdome, honour, pleasure:\\
When almost all was out, God made a stay,\\
Perceiving that alone of all his treasure\\
\vin Rest in the bottome lay.\\

For if I should (said he)\\
Bestow this jewell also on my creature,\\
He would adore my gifts in stead of me,\\
And rest in Nature, not the God of Nature:\\
\vin So both should losers be.\\

Yet let him keep the rest,\\
But keep them with repining restlesnesse:\\
Let him be rich and wearie, that at least,\\
If goodnesse leade him not, yet wearinesse\\
\vin May tosse him to my breast.\\
\end{poem}
\tailpiece

\poemtitle{Prayer}
\poemblock{The six-daies world transposing in an houre,}
\begin{poem}[Prayer the Churches banquet, Angels age,]
Prayer the Churches banquet, Angels age,\\
Gods breath in man returning to his birth,\\
The soul in paraphrase, heart in pilgrimage,\\
The Christian plummet sounding heav'n and earth;\\

Engine against th' Almightie, sinners towre,\\
Reversed thunder, Christ-side-piercing spear,\\
The six-daies world transposing in an houre,\\
A kinde of tune, which all things heare and fear;\\

Softnesse, and peace, and joy, and love, and blisse,\\
Exalted Manna, gladnesse of the best,\\
Heaven in ordinarie, man well drest,\\
The milkie way, the bird of Paradise,\\

Church-bels beyond the starres heard, the souls bloud,\\
The land of spices; something understood.\\
\end{poem}
\tailpiece

\poemtitle{Easter Wings}
\poemblock{Then shall the fall further the flight in me.}
\begin{poem*}[Lord, who createdst man in wealth and store,]
Lord, who createdst man in wealth and store,\\
Though foolishly he lost the same,\\
Decaying more and more,\\
Till he became\\
Most poore:\\
With thee\\
O let me rise\\
As larks, harmoniously,\\
And sing this day thy victories:\\
Then shall the fall further the flight in me.\\

My tender age in sorrow did beginne:\\
And still with sicknesses and shame\\
Thou didst so punish sinne,\\
That I became\\
Most thinne.\\
With thee\\
Let me combine,\\
And feel this day thy victorie:\\
For, if I imp my wing on thine,\\
Affliction shall advance the flight in me.\\
\end{poem*}
\attribution{The Temple: Sacred Poems and Private Ejaculations, Cambridge, 1633.}
