% latex-atlas 09: a page of verse, as a book of poems sets one. The poem is
% a block centred on its longest line, each line flush within it and a
% run-over hanging; a two-line initial opens it; the title in spaced small
% capitals; every fifth line numbered in the margin; the source set small
% and right under the last line, on the block; a tailpiece. Against each, what the
% article class and its verse environment give: a poem indented at the
% left margin, a bold title, no initial, no numbers, the source as a
% paragraph, white space. The text is Herbert's "Vertue" (The Temple,
% 1633), in its spelling; its indented short lines are content, kept in
% every variant.
%
% The verse machinery is the plate's own, not verse.sty's (not installed
% here, and its verse environment would replace the kernel's, the honest
% default): every line ends with \\, stanzas are separated by a blank
% line, a stanza is one paragraph so an initial can span two lines, and
% each line is outdented from a hanging \leftskip so that only a run-over
% keeps the hang. See the block's comment for what \\ does and why there
% is no \everypar. sty/atlas-verse.sty's block descends from this one but
% has no minipage, so a poem there can break across leaves; this plate is a
% one-page specimen and keeps its own.

% --- the poem block -----------------------------------------------------------
% Every line ends with \\, stanzas are separated by a blank line, a stanza
% is one paragraph. \\ does the work: it counts the line, sets its number,
% and then either breaks the line -- a \null before the outdent, because
% glue at the head of a line after a forced break is discarded -- or, if a
% blank line follows, ends the stanza and sets the stanza space itself.
% The first line of a stanza is outdented by a negative \parindent. No
% \everypar: any user \everypar, even \relax, loses the \parskip between
% paragraphs in a minipage under the 2021+ kernel, which is why the stanza
% space is explicit here and \parskip is zero.
\makeatletter
\newlength{\poemwidth}
\newlength{\poemhang}\setlength{\poemhang}{1.5em}
\newlength{\stanzaskip}
\newcounter{poemline}
% \poemblock{longest line}: the width the block is measured by.
\newcommand{\poemblock}[1]{\settowidth{\poemwidth}{#1}}
% Every fifth line numbered at the block's right edge, small and quiet; or
% nothing. Set at the line's end, so it needs no hook at its start.
\atlascontrol{numbers}{
  \newcommand{\poemnumber}{%
    \ifnum\numexpr\value{poemline}/5*5\relax=\value{poemline}\relax
      \hskip\z@\@plus1fill\rlap{\hskip1.4em{\tiny\itshape\color{glossink}\arabic{poemline}}}\fi}
}{
  \newcommand{\poemnumber}{}
}
% The block centred on its longest line, as a book of poems sets it; or
% the poem where the class's verse environment puts it: lines at
% \leftmargini (2.5 em) from the left margin, run-overs 1.5 em further,
% and a right margin to match.
\atlascontrol{block}{
  \newcommand{\poemopen}{\begin{center}\begin{minipage}[t]{\poemwidth}}
  \newcommand{\poemclose}{\end{minipage}\end{center}}
}{
  \newcommand{\poemopen}{\par\noindent\hspace*{\leftmargini}\begin{minipage}[t]{\dimexpr\linewidth-\leftmargini-\leftmargini-1.5em\relax}}
  \newcommand{\poemclose}{\end{minipage}\par}
}
\def\poem@eol{\@ifnextchar\par{\poem@stanzaend}{\@ifnextchar\end{\par}{\newline\null\hskip-\poemhang\ignorespaces}}}
\def\poem@stanzaend\par{\par\vskip\stanzaskip}
\newenvironment{poem}{%
  \par\addvspace{1.2em}\setcounter{poemline}{0}%
  % The stanza space is a line, measured here, where \baselineskip is the
  % text's (in the preamble it is still zero).
  \setlength{\stanzaskip}{\baselineskip}%
  \poemopen
    \parindent-\poemhang \parskip\z@
    \leftskip\poemhang \rightskip\z@\@plus1fil \parfillskip\z@
    \def\\{\unskip\stepcounter{poemline}\poemnumber\poem@eol}%
    \def\vin{\hspace*{2em}}%
    \ignorespaces}
  {\par\poemclose}
\makeatother

% The title in spaced small capitals, centred, in the rubric; or as the
% class heads a section, bold and flush left.
\atlascontrol{title}{
  \newcommand{\poemtitle}[1]{%
    \par\addvspace{1em}\centerline{\small\textcolor{rubric}{\textls[130]{\textsc{#1}}}}\par}
}{
  \newcommand{\poemtitle}[1]{\par\addvspace{1em}{\noindent\Large\bfseries #1\par}}
}
% A two-line initial on the first line; or the first letter as set. Not
% lettrine, whose own \par and \noindent throw away the block's outdent:
% the first two lines hang by the initial's width (\hangafter=-2, which
% composes with the block's \leftskip and per-line outdent on every line),
% the letter is sized by measurement so its height spans the two lines,
% and is set into the hang on the second line's baseline; the rest of the
% word in small capitals, as lettrine sets it.
\makeatletter
\newsavebox{\ini@box}\newlength{\ini@target}
\newcommand{\ini@findent}{0.3em}
\atlascontrol{initial}{
  \newcommand{\incipit}[2]{%
    \leavevmode
    \settoheight{\ini@target}{#1}%
    \setlength{\ini@target}{\dimexpr\baselineskip+1.05\ini@target\relax}%
    \sbox\ini@box{\fontsize{10pt}{10pt}\selectfont #1}%
    \sbox\ini@box{\fontsize{\fpeval{10*\strip@pt\ini@target/\strip@pt\dimexpr\ht\ini@box\relax}pt}{0pt}\selectfont\color{rubric}#1}%
    \hangindent\dimexpr\wd\ini@box+\ini@findent\relax \hangafter-2
    \llap{\smash{\raisebox{-\baselineskip}{\usebox\ini@box}}\hskip\ini@findent}%
    {\scshape #2}}
}{
  \newcommand{\incipit}[2]{#1#2}
}
\makeatother
% The source under the last line, small, italic, set right; or the same
% words as a paragraph.
\atlascontrol{attribution}{
  \newcommand{\attribution}[1]{\par\vspace{0.6em}\begin{center}\begin{minipage}{\poemwidth}\raggedleft\small\itshape #1\par\end{minipage}\end{center}}
}{
  \newcommand{\attribution}[1]{\par\vspace{0.6em}{\noindent #1\par}}
}
% A tailpiece after the poem; or the white space alone.
\atlascontrol{tailpiece}{
  \newcommand{\tailpiece}{\par\vspace{1.4em}\centerline{\pgfornament[width=20mm,color=rubric]{87}}\par}
}{
  \newcommand{\tailpiece}{\par\vspace{1.4em}}
}

\vspace*{6mm}
\poemtitle{Vertue}

\poemblock{Sweet spring, full of sweet dayes and roses,}
\begin{poem}
\incipit{S}{weet} day, so cool, so calm, so bright,\\
The bridall of the earth and skie:\\
The dew shall weep thy fall to night;\\
\vin For thou must die.\\

Sweet rose, whose hue angrie and brave\\
Bids the rash gazer wipe his eye:\\
Thy root is ever in its grave,\\
\vin And thou must die.\\

Sweet spring, full of sweet dayes and roses,\\
A box where sweets compacted lie;\\
My musick shows ye have your closes,\\
\vin And all must die.\\

Onely a sweet and vertuous soul,\\
Like season'd timber, never gives;\\
But though the whole world turn to coal,\\
\vin Then chiefly lives.\\
\end{poem}
\attribution{George Herbert, \textit{The Temple}, 1633.}
\tailpiece

\vfill
\footnotesize\noindent
A poem is a block, not a paragraph: centred on its longest line, each
line flush within it, a run-over hanging, the stanza one paragraph so
that an initial may span two lines. The indented fourth lines are
Herbert's own and are kept in every variant. Numbers every fifth line are
the scholarly edition's; the initial, the rubric and the tailpiece are
the private press's.
