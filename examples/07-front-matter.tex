% A Table of the Propositions: each by its running number and kind, as its
% head reads in the text, with its statement run in and the page set right
% without leaders; or thmtools' list of theorems, which gives number, kind
% and attribution over dot leaders through the same \@dottedtocline that
% \listoffigures uses. \makeatletter must sit outside the control: the
% argument is tokenised before it runs.
\newcounter{propentry}
\newcommand{\abbrTheorem}{Theor.}
\newcommand{\abbrLemma}{Lem.}
\newcommand{\abbrProblem}{Prob.}
\makeatletter
\atlascontrol{table}{
  \newcommand{\tablehead}{%
    \par\addvspace{1.6em}%
    \centerline{\small\textls[130]{\textsc{A Table of the Propositions.}}}%
    \nobreak\vspace{0.7em}}
  % #1 head as set, #2 head as the modern list names it, #3 text, #4 page.
  % The statement keeps clear of the number column: every line is set
  % short by \rightskip, and \parfillskip=-\rightskip lets the last one
  % run out to the edge, where \hfill puts the page. The same idiom as
  % \@dottedtocline, without its dots.
  \NewDocumentCommand{\tableline}{m m m m}{%
    {\par\noindent\hangindent=1.5em\hangafter=1
     \rightskip=2.55em plus 1em \parfillskip=-\rightskip
     \textls[70]{\textsc{#1}}\ \textit{#3}%
     \nobreak\hfill\nobreak\hbox to 1.55em{\hfil #4}\par}%
    \vspace{0.3em}}
  % #1 kind, #2 kind number, #3 attribution, #4 statement, #5 page
  \NewDocumentCommand{\propentry}{m m m m m}{%
    \stepcounter{propentry}%
    \tableline{Prop.~\Roman{propentry}.\ \csname abbr#1\endcsname~#2.}{#1}{#4}{#5}}
}{
  \newcommand{\tablehead}{\section*{List of Theorems}}
  \NewDocumentCommand{\tableline}{m m m m}{%
    \@dottedtocline{1}{1.5em}{2.3em}{#2}{#4}}
  \NewDocumentCommand{\propentry}{m m m m m}{%
    \stepcounter{propentry}%
    \@dottedtocline{1}{1.5em}{2.3em}{\numberline{\arabic{propentry}}#1 (#3)}{#5}}
}
\makeatother

\titleblock

\begin{Argument}
That the pursuit of a robber by cops upon the vertices of a graph, each
moving by turns along its edges, is decided by the figure of the graph
alone. That one cop suffices exactly when the graph may be dismantled
corner by corner (Prop.~I.); that the number of cops needful cannot grow
in passing to a retract (Prop.~II.); that three suffice upon any graph
drawn in the plane without crossing, and that upon a graph without short
circuits no fewer than the least degree will serve (Props.~III., IV.);
and that upon a surface of genus $g$, $\lfloor 3g/2 \rfloor + 3$ are
enough (Prop.~V.). Lastly, of Meyniel's question, whether some multiple of
the square root of the number of vertices suffices for every connected
graph, which is set forth with what is known toward it (Prop.~VI. and
Scholium).
\end{Argument}

\fleuron

\tablehead
\tableline{Definitions.}{Definitions}{Of corners, of dismantling, and of the cop number.}{1}
\propentry{Theorem}{I}{Nowakowski and Winkler; Quilliot, 1983}{A finite connected graph is cop-win if and only if it is dismantlable.}{1}
\propentry{Lemma}{I}{Berarducci and Intrigila, 1993}{If $H$ is a retract of $G$, then $c(H) \le c(G)$.}{1}
\propentry{Theorem}{II}{Aigner and Fromme, 1984}{Three cops suffice upon every planar graph.}{2}
\propentry{Lemma}{II}{Aigner and Fromme, 1984}{Upon a graph of girth five or more, no fewer cops than its least degree will serve.}{2}
\propentry{Theorem}{III}{Schröder, 2001}{Upon a graph of genus $g$, $\lfloor 3g/2 \rfloor + 3$ cops suffice.}{3}
\propentry{Problem}{I}{Meyniel, 1985}{Whether there is an absolute constant $C$ such that $c(G) \le C\sqrt{n}$ for every connected graph upon $n$ vertices.}{4}
\tableline{Scholium.}{Remark}{Of the bounds yet demonstrated toward the Problem.}{4}

\fleuron

The leaf before a treatise of which 04 shews a single page, on the same
stock; 04 numbers its three propositions to stand alone, and this Table
